Incident log

Bugs, data outages, and manual interventions, kept on the public record. A track record that never admits anything is a marketing document, not a record.

OCT 27 · duplicate buys

AUD_USD bought twice on the same breakout signal, doubling intended size. First observed instance of the position-fetch failure pattern described below.

NOV 3 · corrupted exit record

The AUD_USD exit was recorded at price 0.0: the price fetch failed and the failure leaked into the fill record instead of stopping it. The position closed, the record stayed broken. Kept as-is.

NOV 5 · duplicate buys

GBP_USD bought eight times on one signal, about 8,000 units against an intended 1,000. Position manually reduced to intended size.

NOV 12 to 16 · duplicate buys

USD_CAD bought eleven times across five days, about 11,000 units against an intended 1,000. Manually trimmed to 1,000 on Nov 16. Root cause identified: when the position query failed, the bot treated the failure as no position and bought again on the next scan. Fail-safe logic rewritten and deployed Nov 16: a failed fetch now means do nothing, and oversized stacks block new entries. The fill database was deliberately left exactly as it happened.

Full write-up of the incident series: the journal, Nov 29.

Methodology

All entries and exits are generated by static Turtle-style trend-following rules: enter on a 20-day high breakout, exit when price crosses below the 10-day low. The operator does not intervene in entries or exits. Where manual action was unavoidable, it appears above, in the incident log. Position size is about $1,000 per position, maximum three per market, on paper and practice accounts only. One ruleset, three venues: stocks, forex, and crypto. Every closed trade above is auditable against three independent sources: the fill database, the bot logs, and the broker statements.
